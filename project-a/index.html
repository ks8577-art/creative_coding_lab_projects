Krakel

Scientific Name:

Krakelus singularis (Krakel)

Discovered by / at:

Karanjot Singh, during a deep-space survey near the edge of the Violet Nebula, Fall 2026

Family:

Vortacephalidae, the "vortex-heads." Krakel is the only known member. Its closest relatives are thought to be ancient cephalopods (octopuses and squids) that left the oceans and adapted to the vacuum of space millions of years ago.

Habitat:

Open space near colorful nebulas. Krakel drifts slowly through the upper reaches of a star field and is rarely seen lower down, where it seems to avoid the light of passing ships.

Appearance:

Krakel has a small, squat, lumpy head the color of deep bruised violet, marked with darker blotches and thin red veins. A heavy brow hangs over its single eye: a narrow, blood-red eyeball with a glowing toxic-green iris and a black slit pupil. Below it, a wide mouth with a severe overbite holds a single row of yellowed fangs, longest in the middle. Three drooping jowls hang beneath the mouth, trailing a curtain of thin strands tipped in red.

Six long octopus-like tentacles spread from its body, ridged and lined with pale suckers, their tips fading to blood red. Two smaller arms rise from the corners of its mouth and arch over its head, ending in bone-white hooked claws. Small green lights pulse across its skin like a deep-sea creature.

Key Characteristics:

  • Bends space around itself like a black hole.
  • Pulls in starlight and nebula gas as it passes.
  • Its eye follows any movement.
  • Fires red bolts of energy at what it sees.
  • Blinks about every four seconds.
  • Weak to blue light.

What we know so far about Krakel? Long before anyone had seen Krakel, star pilots told stories about patches of space where "the stars don't sit still." Ships passing through the Violet Nebula reported starlight twisting into spirals and clouds of gas being dragged out of shape, as though something invisible were pulling on them. Most of these reports were blamed on faulty instruments.

The first clear sighting came during Karanjot Singh's survey in Fall 2026. Instruments picked up a moving gravitational distortion, and at its center was a living creature. Researchers now believe Krakel produces a small, contained gravity well, similar to a miniature black hole, that bends the light of nearby stars and pulls nebula gas toward it. When Krakel moves on, the space around it relaxes and the stars return to their normal positions. Some scientists think Krakel feeds on the energy of this bent light, which would explain why it is always found near bright nebulas.

Krakel is not known to attack unless it notices movement. Its single eye tracks anything that moves, and after a short pause, it fires focused red bolts of energy from its mouth toward the target. It does not chase. It hovers in the upper part of its territory and lets its tentacles drift around it, constantly reaching in different directions. Its two clawed arms flex slowly open and closed, as if testing the space around it.

Pilots tell a tale that Krakel can be driven off with light of the right color. Blue beams fired upward from below appear to wound it, and after enough hits it is said to vanish from the nebula entirely. Nobody knows whether it is destroyed or simply leaves to find calmer space.

Guidelines for human interaction with this creature
You are the small ship. Move your mouse to fly. Krakel's eye will follow you wherever you go.
Use the first moments wisely. When an encounter begins, Krakel needs a few seconds to notice you before it starts firing.
Dodge the red bolts. Krakel aims at where you are at the moment it fires, so keep moving. A bolt that reaches you costs one heart. You have 3.
Fight back with blue light. Click to fire a blue beam straight up. Krakel can take 10 hits before it retreats.
Watch your hearts and Krakel's health in the top-left corner.
If you lose all your hearts, the encounter ends. Click to try again. If you drive Krakel away, click to face it once more.
